You’re observing a confusing result where the average accuracy is above 80%, but the global accuracy remains stuck at 21%. Here’s what’s happening:

Average Accuracy (>80%): This is the mean of each client’s individual accuracy on their own local test data. Think of it as asking 10 students how they did on their own practice tests, then averaging their scores.

Global Accuracy (21%): This measures the performance of the combined global model on a centralised test set — like testing the entire class’s collective knowledge on a standardised exam.

The main issue here is that, although clients are performing well locally, the process of aggregating their models into a reliable global model is failing. This could be due to:

Trust Filtering — The trust mechanism in TRUST_MCNet may be rejecting too many client updates, preventing the global model from actually learning new patterns.

Data Distribution Mismatch — The global test set may differ significantly from the local data each client is training on, leading to poor generalisation.
